# Comparison Specifications — CompareForge.online

This document defines the standard structure and requirements for comparison pages on CompareForge.

## Purpose of a Comparison Page

A CompareForge comparison page exists to help users understand the real differences between two products. It should answer:

1. What are these two products?
2. How do their specifications compare?
3. What do those specifications actually mean in practice?
4. Which product is better suited for different use cases?
5. What are the trade-offs?
6. What should the user consider before deciding?

The page must NOT simply list specifications. It must explain them.

## Standard Comparison Page Structure

### 1. H1 Title

**Format:** `Product A vs Product B`

Or an appropriate variation that clearly identifies the comparison.

**Rules:**
- One H1 per page
- Must include both product names
- Must be clear and specific
- No keyword stuffing

### 2. Introduction

**Purpose:** Set context for the comparison.

**Content:**
- What products are being compared (full names, brands)
- What category they belong to
- What this comparison covers
- Who this comparison is for
- Brief note on what users will learn

**Length:** 2-4 sentences. Not a long paragraph.

**Rules:**
- No generic filler text
- Must add value, not just repeat the title
- No keyword stuffing

### 3. Quick Comparison

**Purpose:** Give users an immediate overview.

**Content:**
- Side-by-side summary of the most important differences
- Key specifications at a glance
- Price range (if available and sourced)
- One-line summary of each product's main strength

**Format:** Usually a small table or two-column layout.

**Rules:**
- Must be scannable in 10 seconds
- Must show the most important differences immediately
- No excessive detail here

### 4. Key Differences

**Purpose:** Highlight the 3-5 most important differences.

**Content:**
- Each difference explained in plain language
- What each difference means for the user
- Why it matters

**Format:** Usually 3-5 points, each with a brief explanation.

**Rules:**
- Focus on differences that actually matter
- No trivial differences listed as key
- Plain language, no jargon without explanation
- Evidence-based

### 5. Detailed Comparison

**Purpose:** Provide thorough feature-by-feature analysis.

**Content:**
- Feature-by-feature breakdown
- For each feature:
  - What each product offers
  - What the difference means in practice
  - Why it matters (or doesn't) for different users

**Format:** Organized by feature/topic, usually with H2 or H3 headings.

**Rules:**
- Must add analysis, not just list specs
- Must explain technical terms
- Must be organized logically
- Must be thorough but not padded

### 6. Specification Table

**Purpose:** Present specifications in a scannable, structured format.

**Content:**
- Side-by-side specifications
- Organized by category (e.g., Design, Performance, Features, etc.)
- Only include specifications relevant to the comparison
- Technical terms explained where needed

**Format:** Responsive table.

**Rules:**
- Must be responsive (work on mobile)
- Must be labeled clearly
- Must not include dozens of irrelevant specifications
- Must explain technical specifications where users may not understand them
- Must be sourced (footnote or inline)

### 7. Use-Case Analysis

**Purpose:** Help users decide based on their specific needs.

**Content:**
- Scenario-based recommendations
- "If you need X, Product A may be better because..."
- "If you prioritize Y, Product B might be more suitable because..."
- Trade-offs for each scenario

**Format:** Usually organized by use case or user type.

**Rules:**
- No absolute "winner" declarations
- Use conditional language: "may be better," "might be more suitable"
- Focus on trade-offs, not recommendations
- Evidence-based
- Multiple scenarios, not just one

### 8. Advantages and Disadvantages

**Purpose:** Provide an honest assessment of each product.

**Content:**
- Advantages of Product A
- Disadvantages of Product A
- Advantages of Product B
- Disadvantages of Product B

**Format:** Two lists per product, or side-by-side.

**Rules:**
- Honest and balanced
- Based on evidence
- Not biased by affiliate commissions
- Include both pros and cons for each product
- Specific, not generic

### 9. Important Considerations

**Purpose:** Surface factors users might overlook.

**Content:**
- Compatibility issues
- Regional availability differences
- Support and warranty differences
- Long-term considerations
- Limitations of the comparison
- Factors that may change over time

**Format:** Bullet points or short paragraphs.

**Rules:**
- Only include genuinely important considerations
- No filler

### 10. Methodology / Sources

**Purpose:** Be transparent about where information comes from.

**Content:**
- Where specifications were sourced
- How information was verified
- Date of last update
- Limitations of the data
- Any assumptions made

**Format:** Short section at the bottom.

**Rules:**
- Must be present on every comparison
- Must be honest
- Must cite sources
- Must acknowledge limitations

### 11. FAQ

**Purpose:** Answer common questions users might have.

**Content:**
- 3-5 genuinely useful questions
- Clear, helpful answers
- Questions users actually search for

**Format:** Q&A format, usually with schema markup.

**Rules:**
- Only include genuinely useful FAQs
- No obvious or trivial questions
- No keyword-stuffed questions
- Must add value beyond the main comparison content

### 12. Related Comparisons

**Purpose:** Help users discover other relevant comparisons.

**Content:**
- 2-4 related comparisons
- Within the same category or related categories
- Brief context for why they might be relevant

**Format:** Card or link format.

**Rules:**
- Must be contextually relevant
- Must not be random
- Must link to actual, existing pages

### 13. Related Guides

**Purpose:** Connect comparison to educational content.

**Content:**
- 1-2 related guides
- Brief context for relevance

**Format:** Card or link format.

**Rules:**
- Must be genuinely related
- Must link to actual, existing pages

## Language and Tone Rules

### Do NOT Use Winner Language

Instead of:
- "Product A is the best"
- "Product A wins"
- "Product A is the clear winner"
- "We recommend Product A"

Use:
- "Product A may be better suited for..."
- "Product A offers more of..."
- "Product A has an advantage in..."
- "Users who prioritize X might prefer Product A"
- "Product A is a better fit if..."
- "Product B may be preferable for users who..."

### Do NOT Use Marketing Language

Instead of:
- "Amazing performance"
- "Incredible value"
- "Revolutionary features"
- "Unbeatable quality"

Use:
- Specific, factual descriptions
- Measurable differences
- Concrete examples
- Evidence-based claims

### Do NOT Make Unsupported Claims

Instead of:
- "This is the best product in its class"
- "This product outperforms everything else"
- "Users love this product"

Use:
- "According to [source], this product..."
- "Specifications indicate..."
- "Based on available data..."
- "This product offers [specific feature] which..."

## Content Rules

### Minimum Requirements for a Comparison Page

- Introduction that sets context
- Quick comparison overview
- At least 3 key differences explained
- Detailed comparison of major features
- Specification table with relevant specs
- Use-case analysis with multiple scenarios
- Honest advantages/disadvantages for both products
- Methodology and sources section
- Related content links

### What NOT to Include

- Fake testing or hands-on experience
- Fake benchmarks or performance data
- Invented user reviews
- Unsupported claims
- Keyword stuffing
- Repetitive filler paragraphs
- Copied manufacturer descriptions
- Fake "winner" declarations

### Evidence Standards

- Specifications: cite the source (manufacturer, retailer, or independent review)
- Performance claims: cite the source or testing methodology
- Price: note that prices change and provide the date checked
- Features: verify against manufacturer specifications
- Claims: back with evidence or qualify as uncertain

## Template Adaptation

The standard structure above is a guide. Not every comparison must follow it exactly.

Adapt the structure based on:

- The specific products being compared
- The most important differences
- User search intent
- The natural flow of the comparison

Do NOT force a template onto a comparison where it doesn't make sense.

For example:
- If specifications are the main difference, lead with the specification table
- If use cases are the main factor, lead with use-case analysis
- If the products are very similar, focus on the few meaningful differences
- If the products are very different, structure the comparison accordingly

The template serves the content. The content does not serve the template.
