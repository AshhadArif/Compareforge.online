# Editorial Guidelines — CompareForge.online

## Purpose

These guidelines ensure all CompareForge content is accurate, honest, useful, and trustworthy. Every piece of content must meet these standards before publication.

## Core Principles

### 1. Accuracy First

- All facts must be verifiable
- All specifications must be sourced
- All claims must be evidence-based
- Correct errors promptly when found

### 2. Honesty Always

- Do not fabricate testing or experience
- Do not claim expertise you do not have
- Do not make unsupported claims
- Acknowledge limitations and uncertainty

### 3. User Value

- Every page must help the reader
- Content must be useful independent of advertising
- Comparisons must save users time and effort
- Analysis must explain what specifications mean in practice

### 4. Transparency

- Sources must be documented
- Methodology must be explained
- Limitations must be acknowledged
- Updates must be noted

---

## Writing Standards

### Tone

- Professional but accessible
- Clear and direct
- Neutral and balanced
- Helpful without being condescending

### Language

- Plain language, not jargon
- Technical terms explained when used
- Short sentences and paragraphs
- Active voice preferred
- No filler text or padding

### Structure

- Clear headings and subheadings
- Bullet points for lists
- Tables for specifications
- Scannable layout
- Mobile-friendly formatting

## Comparison Writing Rules

### Do NOT Use

**Winner language:**
- "Product A is the best"
- "Product A wins"
- "We recommend Product A"
- "The clear winner is..."
- "Product A beats Product B"

**Marketing language:**
- "Amazing performance"
- "Incredible value"
- "Revolutionary features"
- "Unbeatable quality"
- "Game-changing"

**Fake expertise:**
- "In our testing..."
- "We found that..."
- "Our experience showed..."
- (unless actual testing was conducted)

**Unsupported claims:**
- "This is the most popular..."
- "Users love this product..."
- "This is the best-selling..."
- (unless backed by verifiable data)

### DO Use

**Conditional language:**
- "Product A may be better suited for..."
- "Product A offers more of..."
- "Users who prioritize X might prefer..."
- "Product A has an advantage in..."
- "Product B may be preferable if..."

**Evidence-based language:**
- "According to [source]..."
- "Specifications indicate..."
- "Based on available data..."
- "The manufacturer states..."

**Balanced language:**
- "Product A excels at X, while Product B is stronger in Y"
- "Trade-offs include..."
- "The main differences are..."
- "Both products have strengths in different areas"

---

## What NOT to Write

### No Fabricated Testing

If you have not physically tested a product:
- Do NOT write "In our testing..."
- Do NOT write "We found that..."
- Do NOT write "Our experience showed..."
- Do NOT write "After using this product..."
- Do NOT write "In our hands-on time..."

Instead:
- "According to reviews from..."
- "Specifications suggest..."
- "Independent tests indicate..."
- "The manufacturer claims..."

### No Fabricated Ratings

- Do NOT create fake star ratings
- Do NOT invent review scores
- Do NOT make up user satisfaction percentages
- Do NOT fabricate benchmark results

### No Fabricated Experience

- Do NOT write as if you have used a product you have not used
- Do NOT invent personal experiences with products
- Do NOT create fictional scenarios as if they were real
- Do NOT claim to have compared products in person unless you have

### No Fabricated Expertise

- Do NOT claim to be a product expert unless you are
- Do NOT claim professional testing capabilities unless you have them
- Do NOT claim laboratory testing unless it was actually conducted
- Do NOT claim to have industry connections unless you do

## Specification Rules

### Listing Specifications

- Source every specification
- Use manufacturer data as primary source
- Cross-reference with independent sources
- Note any discrepancies
- Update when corrections are found

### Explaining Specifications

- Explain what each specification means
- Explain why it matters (or doesn't)
- Use plain language
- Provide context for technical terms
- Compare to competitors where relevant

### Avoiding Specification Errors

- Do not copy specifications without verification
- Do not assume specifications from one model apply to another
- Do not mix up model variants
- Do not use outdated specifications without noting the date
- Do not invent specifications that cannot be verified

## Content Types

### Comparison Pages

Follow the structure in COMPARISON-SPECIFICATIONS.md.

Requirements:
- Original analysis, not just specification listing
- Honest advantages and disadvantages
- Multiple use-case scenarios
- Evidence-based conclusions
- Source documentation

### Guide Pages

Requirements:
- Clear explanation of the topic
- Practical value for the reader
- Links to relevant comparisons
- No padding or filler
- Original content

### Category Pages

Requirements:
- Useful overview of the category
- Clear navigation to comparisons
- Buying considerations
- No thin content

## Editorial Review Process

### Before Publication

1. **Accuracy check:** Verify all facts and specifications
2. **Source check:** Ensure all sources are documented
3. **Originality check:** Ensure content is original, not copied
4. **Value check:** Ensure the page provides genuine value
5. **Tone check:** Ensure tone is professional and balanced
6. **Grammar check:** Ensure proper grammar and spelling
7. **Link check:** Ensure all links work
8. **Mobile check:** Ensure page works on mobile
9. **SEO check:** Ensure proper structure and metadata

### Ongoing Review

- Monthly: Check for outdated information
- Quarterly: Review content quality
- When errors reported: Correct promptly
- When products discontinued: Mark as outdated

---

## Specific Content Rules

### Price Information

- Always note when prices were last checked
- Always note that prices may vary
- Do not claim "lowest price"
- Do not invent historical prices
- Use ranges when prices vary by retailer

### Availability Information

- Note if product is current or discontinued
- Note regional availability differences
- Update when products are discontinued

### Feature Claims

- Verify features against manufacturer specifications
- Note any limitations or caveats
- Do not overstate feature capabilities
- Explain what features actually do in practice

### Comparison Scope

- Compare products that make sense to compare
- Do not compare products at vastly different price points unless intentional
- Do not compare products from different categories
- Do not create comparisons for products that are too similar to differentiate

---

## Quality Standards

### Minimum Requirements

- All facts verified
- All sources documented
- Original analysis present
- Useful to the reader
- Properly structured
- No errors found during review
- Mobile responsive
- All links working

### Rejection Criteria

The following content must NOT be published:
- Fabricated testing or experience claims
- Unsupported "best product" claims
- Copied content from other sources
- Thin content with no original value
- Keyword-stuffed content
- Content that fails accuracy checks
- Content with broken links
- Content not mobile responsive

---

## Corrections

### When Errors Are Found

1. Correct the error promptly
2. Note the correction on the page (if appropriate)
3. Update the "Last updated" date
4. If the error was significant, note what was corrected

### User-Reported Errors

1. Acknowledge the report
2. Verify the error
3. Correct if valid
4. Thank the reporter
5. Update the page
